## Cuerpo (inglés)

My portfolio's home page is a still system around a black hole, Gargantua,
with five bodies around it: two ships, two planets and a tesseract. You can
open each one full screen here: https://jonasjavier.dev/en/experiments

There isn't a single `THREE.Light` in the scene. The only light source is the
accretion disk at the origin, and every body is lit from there in one shared
fragment shader:

- **Key:** the disk's amber, `vec3(1.0, 0.78, 0.52)`.
- **Fill:** `vec3(0.044, 0.058, 0.115)`. The sky, not a second lamp.
- **Terminator:** `smoothstep(-0.08, 0.34, n·l + relief)`.

The nice side effect is that placement and lighting can't disagree: move a
ship around the black hole and its lit side follows.

The metals also reflect the disk without a cubemap. The environment here is
simple enough to write down: a huge amber ring hugging the orbital plane, and
black everywhere else. So the reflection is a function of the reflected view
ray:

```glsl
vec3 reflDir = reflect(-view, normal);
float reflAlign = max(dot(reflDir, toLight), 0.0);
float envBand = mix(1.0, 0.24, smoothstep(0.12, 0.70, abs(reflDir.y)));
float envSheet = (pow(reflAlign, 2.5) * 0.38 + pow(reflAlign, 14.0) * 0.85)
               * envBand;
```

A wide lobe (the disk covers a lot of sky), a hot core, squashed vertically
because the disk lives on its plane. Unlike diffuse, it doesn't depend on the
day side, so the night side of a hull can still catch the disk.

Every body has to pass a bloom-off test: bloom and emissives at zero, and it
still has to read as silhouette, volume and material.

What isn't solved: MSAA 4x only runs on a real GPU. With software rendering
the ships' edges come back as stairs, so I can't judge a pass from CI
captures.

Question: for a scene with one dominant, describable light source, would you
go further with analytic environment terms, or is a tiny baked cubemap the
saner path at some point?
