  seoTitle: "Tesseract — Interactive 4D Hypercube in WebGL"
  seoDescription: "A tesseract, the four-dimensional hypercube inspired by Interstellar: sixteen vertices rotating through the fourth dimension, as glass in real time with WebGL."
  notes:
    - "Sixteen vertices numbered 0 to 15: each of their four bits says which side they sit on in X, Y, Z and W, and two vertices share an edge when they differ in a single bit. That gives 32 edges, 24 faces and 8 cells."
    - "It rotates in the XW, YW and ZW planes, the ones that mix our space with the fourth dimension, and is projected to 3D with perspective from W: depth in W shares thickness and light between the near cell and the far one."
    - "Four Three.js meshes share a single GLSL shader: six-sided glass tubes with a Fresnel glow, the light tip and its trail in additive blending, six glass panes and an occluder that only writes depth."
  registro:
    intencion: >-
      To find out whether a four-dimensional shape could read as an object
      rather than a diagram.
    prueba: >-
      The first version was mathematically correct and impossible to read:
      thirty-two glass edges the eye couldn't sort into depth.
    construccion: >-
      Sixteen vertices rotating across four planes and projected in
      perspective, traced by a light that completes an Eulerian circuit. Four
      draw calls and one shared material.
    resultado: >-
      Legibility didn't come from exposure but from hierarchy. Each vertex
      publishes its depth in the fourth dimension, and that value splits light
      and thickness between the hypercube's two cells: thick and bright up
      close, thin and dim far away.
    iteraciones: >-
      The frame corridor, the compact box and a brighter exposure were all
      thrown out. What worked was the opposite: dimming the far cell.
