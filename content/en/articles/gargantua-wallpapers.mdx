Gargantua, the black hole in *Interstellar*, is the image I spent the most time
looking at while building this site. It lives in
[my simulator](/en/experiments/observatory/gargantua), which draws it in real
time in the browser by following light around the horizon. These wallpapers
come from there: not stretched screenshots or illustrations, but the simulator
rendering at a true 3840 × 2160 pixels, with the interface switched off.

Tap any image to see it full size, or use the button to download it.

## How they were made

Each image is a frame from the simulator, not an hours-long render. The shader
follows one ray of light per pixel, bends it around the black hole and checks
whether it falls into the horizon or escapes; while the view is still it keeps
adding frames and the image sharpens on its own. For the wallpapers I opened
the simulator in a window exactly the size of 4K, picked each of its four
views, hid the interface and waited for the image to settle.

The Doppler effect is on in all of them but one: a real disk is brighter on
the side coming towards you, and the film chose to leave that out. I explain
it properly in [the physics of Interstellar's black hole](/en/blog/interstellar-black-hole-gargantua-explained),
and the code is in [how I built a black hole in WebGL](/en/blog/how-i-built-a-black-hole-in-webgl).
If you'd rather see it move, [step into the simulator](/en/experiments/observatory/gargantua)
and pick the framing yourself.
